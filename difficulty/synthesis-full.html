What is the difficulty of a task?

Rebuilt 15 Sep 13:23 UK; data as of 15 Sep 13:23 UK; rebuilt with every new count landing for the original tasks and after any edit to the text.

Synthesis of an 18-field literature sweep, 11 measure-generation lenses with adversarial critique, and empirical checks on local eval data. Supporting material: notes/lit/ (per-field notes), MEASURES.md (critiqued catalog), notes/empirical-facts.md (local data illustrations), notes/deep-reads.md (method extractions).

Terms used here (plain language). IRT, item response theory, the psychometrics of standardized testing: solvers get ability numbers, tasks get difficulty numbers, estimated jointly from who-solved-what. 2PL: the standard IRT model; each task gets b (difficulty: the capability at which success reaches 50%) and a (discrimination: how sharply success flips as capability rises); low/negative a marks broken or lure tasks; θ is solver capability on the same scale. CTT p-value, classical test theory's difficulty: plain fraction-correct in a population. Elo / Glicko / WHR, chess-style rating systems: solvers and tasks rated on one scale from win/loss records. pass@k / pass^k: chance a task is solved at least once in k tries / all k times. GLAD: a crowdsourcing model where item difficulty multiplies (rather than adds to) solver ability, so truly ambiguous items drive everyone to chance. DIF, differential item functioning: two solver populations ordering the same tasks differently. the difficulty scale stretches fail rates at both ends so that very easy and very hard tasks stop bunching together (in symbols, log(p/(1−p))).

1. The object itself

Across every field that has operationalized it (psychometrics, medicine, learning theory, search/planning, economics, forecasting, sport) the same answer recurs: difficulty is not a property of a task. It is a property of an encounter between

  • a task s (a specification, including its inputs and latent state),
  • a solver population P (with its abilities, priors, and architecture),
  • a protocol R (resources, affordances, attempts, tools, help available),
  • a verifier V (what counts as success, at what strictness).

The primitive object is the success-probability surface p(s, P, R, V). Every difficulty measure in the catalog is a functional of this surface, and every fight about "the" difficulty of a task is a fight about which functional and which frame (P, R, V) got fixed silently. Fields that look like they define intrinsic difficulty are, on inspection, doing one of two things: defining a predictor of the encounter (structural complexity), or taking a limit/supremum over frames (worst-case complexity, instance complexity's machine-independence up to constants).

Witnesses from opposite corners: IRT identifies item difficulty only jointly with solver ability, up to scale (Rasch 1960; De Boeck & Wilson 2004). Learning theory makes the frame explicit as a triple: DFAs are cryptographically hard from random examples, polynomial with membership queries, trivial from a helpful teacher (Angluin; Goldman & Kearns). Chess positions with shallow traps are hard for MCTS and trivial for minimax (Ramanujan et al. 2010). In our own data, per-task pass rates of sizes of the same model family rank-correlate at only ρ≈0.74–0.89; "the" ordering does not exist even inside Qwen2.5-Coder. And verifier strictness is part of the frame: EvalPlus base-vs-plus tests, or HumanEval's ~30% false-positive rate under weak tests (AlphaCode), move "difficulty" without touching the task.

The definitional core, distilled from the philosophy literature (von Kriegstein 2019, against Bradford's effort account): difficulty of succeeding is low probability of success given competent trying; difficulty of doing is the effort/skill the attempt demands. These two families do not reduce to each other, and both are legitimate. Measurement practice mirrors this split: outcome statistics (pass rates, IRT, Elo) estimate the first; effort scales (RVU work = time × intensity, GOMS predicted time, reasoning-token counts) estimate the second.

2. Five functional families

Nearly all 39 cataloged measures are one of five functionals of p:

  1. Location, where the task sits on an outcome scale at a fixed frame: pass/failure rate (CTT p-value), IRT difficulty b estimated jointly with ability, paired-comparison ratings (Elo, Glicko, Whole-History Rating, Bradley-Terry). The gold-standard family; population-relative by construction.
  2. Threshold / titration, the minimum resource at which success reaches a criterion τ: psychophysical staircases (QUEST), pass@k inverted (k needed for 50%), smallest model scale that solves, METR's 50%-time-horizon inversion, age-of-acquisition norms (age by which 50% of children succeed, the developmental time horizon). Converts difficulty into interpretable resource units; requires a monotone resource axis and censors at the ends.
  3. Work / divergence, what the solver must expend or overcome: physician work RVU (negotiated time × intensity), GOMS/KLM predicted execution time, reasoning-token expenditure, and the theoretical form: steering work = −log p₀(success set), the nats separating the solver's prior policy from success (equivalently: description length of the needed correction). The only scale on which independent conjunctive subtasks are additive.
  4. Order position, difficulty with no numeric scale at all: Turing degrees (reducibility partial order; provably incomparable elements), implicational acquisition hierarchies (Guttman scaling; stages instruction cannot reorder), curriculum prerequisite structures. A reminder that forcing a total order onto genuinely partial structure destroys information.
  5. Demand profile, a vector, not a scalar: ADeLe's 18 rubric demand scales, Wood's component/coordinative/dynamic complexity, cognitive load theory's element interactivity, HRA's nominal error rate × performance shaping factors, METR's 16 messiness factors. The decomposed, explanatory family: the bridge from task content to predicted outcomes.

Cross-cutting the families, three distinctions matter everywhere:

  • Ex-post vs ex-ante. Outcome-derived estimates (families 1–3 as usually computed) versus content-derived predictions (family 5, QDE, query performance prediction). Every mature field runs both and tracks their divergence as its own statistic: 93% of difficult intubations were unanticipated (Nørskov); text-based item-difficulty prediction barely beats predict-the-mean in homogeneous populations (BEA 2024 shared task). The honest summary: ex-ante prediction of difficulty is genuinely hard, its ceiling is real, and the residual (what outcomes show that content did not predict) is often the most informative signal.
  • Solving vs acquiring. Learning theory separates the cost of executing a task from the cost of learning it from data: parity is trivial to evaluate and brutal to acquire. For model evals this is the difference between "can the model do it" (pass rate) and "how many demonstrations/gradient steps until it can" (few-shot count, fine-tuning-steps-to-criterion, teaching dimension).
  • Coverage vs reliability. p vs p^k: solving at least once given many tries versus succeeding dependably (pass@k vs pass^k; METR's 50% vs 80% horizons, ~5× apart; medicine's O:E ratios are reliability objects). Tasks and solvers re-rank between these two, so "hard" must declare which one it means. Toby Ord's constant-hazard model connects them: if a task is a chain of fail-any-one steps, log-success is additive in length: length-hazard difficulty, distinct from per-step hardness (and much of the small-vs-large model gap is execution length, not single-step ability; arXiv:2509.09677).

3. What difficulty is NOT (the conflation table)

Each row is a documented failure mode of treating X as difficulty, with the strongest witness found.

Conflation Why it's wrong Witness
Failure rate as such Confounds task demand with population choice, defects, contamination, verifier error. Medicine institutionally refuses raw failure rate; the entire risk-adjustment enterprise (POSSUM, O:E audit) exists to separate case-mix from skill. Our MBPP+ "hardest" tail: underspec/contradiction tasks pass 0.21–0.23 vs tricky 0.47: the bottom of the sort is broken, not hard. GSM1k: up to 13pp of "easiness" was memorization.
Complexity Structural complexity (parts and interdependencies) is a predictor of difficulty, not the thing; org-psych keeps the layers apart explicitly (complexity → performer-relative load → outcomes). Wood 1986; Campbell 1988; Liu & Li 2012. Low-width planning problems are PSPACE-hard in theory, easy in practice (Lipovetzky & Geffner).
Effort or time alone Equal-duration tasks differ in work density; equal-effort tasks differ in success odds. RVU explicitly rejected time-alone (Hsiao 1988); METR: each messiness factor costs ~8pp success at fixed human time; von Kriegstein vs Bradford.
Discrimination / informativeness An item everyone fails carries near-zero information about solvers; hard ≠ useful-for-measurement. "Tricky" items typically surface as low or negative discrimination, not high difficulty. IRT item information peaks at θ≈b (Fisher information); Rodriguez et al. 2021: negative-discrimination items are usually annotation errors.
Ambiguity / ill-posedness Hard drives competent solvers toward chance; ambiguous drives them toward principled disagreement. Different latent structure, different fix (solve harder vs re-specify). GLAD's 1/β vs annotator-disagreement models; ChaosNLI human label variation; our audit's underspecification tag.
Unsolvability Regret-based curricula explicitly separate "hard but attainable" (high regret) from "impossible" (zero regret: no learning value). UED literature; pass@128=0 tasks in our data are candidates for defect screening, not maximal difficulty.
One bad run Solver randomness gives heavy-tailed effort distributions on a fixed instance; difficulty is a distribution property. Gomes et al. 2000 (SAT restarts); our greedy-vs-sampled disagreements (MBPP+ 1.5B: 24 tasks greedy-fail but >50% sampled-pass).
Human difficulty (for model evals) Orderings dissociate in both directions: crowdworker-easy ARC tasks stump frontier models; models do expert-hours math in seconds; anti-prior tasks invert scaling. H-ARC vs frontier models; our HumanEval/124 (Feb-29 rule punishes better calendar priors; 1.5B beats 7B).
Solver deficiency Observed shortfall = task's information scarcity + solver bias + solver noise; only the first is the task's difficulty. BIN model (Satopää et al. 2021) formally decomposes them; SDT separates d′ from criterion.
Emergence threshold "Task needs scale X" is largely a metric artifact; thresholds smooth out under continuous metrics and observational capability axes. Schaeffer et al. 2023 (mirage); Ruan et al. 2024.
Length Length is one demand among many; hazard-per-step × length is a model, not an identity. METR messiness; Ord's constant-hazard as an explicit (falsifiable) assumption.
Acquisition difficulty Hard-to-learn ≠ hard-to-do, in both directions. Kearns-Valiant (easy-to-run, crypto-hard-to-learn); parity.
Stakes / risk Medicine deliberately prices stakes into work (psychological-stress component), but as an explicit, separate decision, not a confusion. RVU intensity decomposition vs POSSUM risk: same field, two currencies, never merged silently.

Two meta-lessons ride on top:

  • Goodhart applies to difficulty scales. When a difficulty measure carries incentives (payment, leaderboards, curricula), it drifts: RVU time estimates exceed logged operating-room times by ~31 min on average; DRG creep; benchmark contamination is the eval-world version. Scheduled empirical re-anchoring is the medicine-tested fix.
  • Difficulty is largely invisible to the solver in-flight. Physician confidence moved 7.2→6.4 while accuracy collapsed 55%→6% across easy→hard vignettes; help-seeking didn't rise. The confidence–accuracy gap, not low confidence, is the signature of a hard case, directly transferable to LLM self-report and calibration-based measures.

4. The measure space (39 measures; full critiqued catalog in MEASURES.md)

The catalog (M01–M39), grouped; every entry has a generic definition, an AI-eval and a non-AI instantiation, and an independent skeptical critique:

  • Outcome statistics and their decompositions: M01 frame-relative failure rate (with the instance-vs-attempt variance decomposition: "all coin-flips" vs "half impossible"), M03 irreducible-failure ceiling (reachable-at-all vs merely rare), M04 mode-distribution discordance (does the solver's own selection find its correct solutions), M05 difficulty-measurement precision (the meta-uncertainty on every other number).
  • Latent-scale psychometrics: M06 IRT ability-location b (human parameterizations: ratings, Angoff, curriculum position; folded in as instantiations), M07 capability slope & diagnosticity (discrimination, transition width), M08 solver-dependence & scalar validity (DIF, dominance-order incomparability, general-factor residual: how far one number can be trusted at all).
  • Resource horizons (titration family): M02 search cost to first success (k50, −log p in nats), M09 scale horizon (smallest capability that reaches criterion), M11 serial deliberation demand, M12 acquisition horizon & run-contingency, M18 adaptation & retention (fine-tuning steps-to-criterion, forgetting half-life), M27 human effort horizon (time & price), M32 hint/scaffold dose to criterion, M33 the universal-solver (Levin) limit as the theoretical anchor.
  • Scaling behavior: M10 inverse scaling & error persistence (difficulty that grows with capability: prior-misalignment).
  • Structural / ex-ante content: M13 solution-object complexity, M14 serial/compositional depth, M15 solution codelength & decision entropy, M16 prompt informativeness & example load, M24 specification & input-domain breadth, M25 knowledge/concept rarity.
  • Error structure (the "tricky" family): M19 error-space multiplicity (one wrong attractor vs scattered failures), M20 lure strength (prior–task misalignment), M21 verifier-strictness / hidden-edge-case share, M22 near-miss repair distance, M23 error deceptiveness & detection cost.
  • Validity screens (gate everything else): M17 shortcut, memorization & surface robustness; M26 specification ambiguity (hard vs underspecified: GLAD-style chance-collapse vs principled disagreement).
  • Judgment & self-knowledge: M29 appraised difficulty (judge & self), M30 confidence–outcome gap (deceptive difficulty; the medical accuracy-confidence dissociation), M31 generation–verification asymmetry (checkability; predicts when best-of-N, delegation, and oversight work).
  • Ecological / agentic: M34 feedback economics & iterative repair, M35 error recoverability, M36 observability gap, M37 environment drift & opposition, M38 coordination load, M39 slack/budget tightness.

Critique outcome: 38/39 keep-with-caveats, 1 fold (M28 human-skill location → into M06). No measure survived without caveats: the modal critic finding is that each measure partly tracks its frame (population, verifier, elicitation protocol) rather than the task, and the modal kill-test is a frame-swap audit: recompute under a second population / verifier / elicitation; if ranks reshuffle, you were measuring the frame. Second cross-cutting finding: within one frame, all location-family numbers (pass rate, b, Elo, −log p) are near-monotone transforms of one measurement; real information gain comes only from adding discrimination, a second frame, an error-structure axis, or an ex-ante axis.

5. If you only keep a handful of numbers per task

Grounded in the per-measure critiques (scores 0–10 in MEASURES.md; top of the list: M01/M02 at 8; M03, M06/M07, M11, M14, M17, M20, M27, M31, M32 at 7):

  1. M01 frame-relative failure rate, with its variance decomposition and an explicit frame card: the anchor scale. (M06/M07 IRT b + a is its population-corrected upgrade once the solver population is wide enough to identify it.)
  2. M02 search cost / −log p̂ per (task, reference solver): continuous, distinguishes rarely- from never-solved, additive over independent subtasks.
  3. One titration on a real resource axis: M27 human time, M09 scale horizon, or M32 hint dose (the causal one; constrain the hint channel: machine teaching's collusion lesson).
  4. One error-structure statistic, M20 lure strength or M19 error-space multiplicity: separates tricky (one wrong attractor) from deep (scattered search failure). M21's verifier-strictness share quantifies the "looks right, fails hidden corners" axis.
  5. One ex-ante demand profile (M14 depth count or an ADeLe-style rubric): the explanatory bridge, published with its measured predictive ceiling against outcomes, which the QDE literature warns is modest.

Gates, not numbers: M17 (contamination/paraphrase screen) and M26 (ambiguity screen) applied before interpreting anything above: the tails of every outcome measure are where broken items pool. And one meta-rule from M05/M08: publish rank confidence and a two-frame rank-correlation audit with any difficulty release, separating identified ranks from prior-driven ones.

6. Mapping to the local project (illustration, not the frame)

The current two measures are both family-1 objects in a narrow frame:

  • Pooled pass rate = the Reference-Frame Failure Rate with P = {Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5B, 3B, 7B}, R = 1 sample at T=0.8 (×384), V = EvalPlus plus-tests. Fine as a workhorse; its known artifacts on this data: censoring (~20–25% of tasks at the 0%/100% ends), defect pooling at the hard tail, equal-weight pooling over models that disagree at ρ≈0.74–0.89.
  • Regular/tricky tag = a coarse ex-ante error-structure judgment. Empirically it is ~89% predictable from pass rate (AUC 0.887/0.890), partly circular (auditors saw pass-rate digests), so today the project effectively has ~1.1 measures, not 2. The tag's non-redundant content is real but thin: the base-pass-plus-fail gap captures a "hidden edge case" axis at AUC ≈0.70 vs the tag, independent of overall rate.

Cheapest high-value upgrades, in dependency order (all computable from existing rollouts except #4): (1) defect-screen-then-sort (already started via filtered plots); (2) per-model −log p̂ vectors + 2PL fit: needs widening the population beyond 3 same-family models to be identified (add other families / sizes; even 10–15 models transforms the fit); (3) failure-mode entropy / lure mass from the stored failing solutions and failing-test identities; (4) a 6–10 dimension ADeLe-style demand rubric scored by an LLM with outcome-validation, replacing the binary tricky tag with named axes (misdirection, hidden-edge-cases, efficiency-demand, knowledge-rarity, spec load, …).

Update: #2 is now piloted. The the maintainers sweep produced a 28-config × 454-task × 128-rollout matrix (Qwen2.5-Coder, Qwen3 ±thinking, Claude ±thinking, DeepSeek), enough to identify a 2PL. Results in analysis/irt/ + notes/irt-pilot.md: Spearman(b, −pooled rate) = 0.87 (the population correction reorders real mass); tricky tasks are less discriminative (mean a 1.59 vs 2.08) and harder, confirming the tag-as-low-discrimination prediction; 18 tasks have a < 0.3 (outcome nearly unrelated to ability: lure/defect audit candidates, unrankable by difficulty); one genuinely anti-ability task found (Mbpp/261, a = −0.9: a tuple-division task whose worked example shows only whole-number results, so weak models apply plain true division / and pass while strong reasoners infer that floor division // was intended and fail the non-exact hidden cases; the pooled rate files this inversion as mid-difficulty). Widening the population also dissolved the saturation problem (1 all-pass task vs ~20% under 3 Qwens). Caveats in the pilot note (2PL misfit, shared-weight configs, protocol differences).

Method note: 18 field sweeps (web-verified citations), 11 generation lenses producing 130 raw candidate measures → clustered into 39 canonical measures → one full entry + one independent skeptical critique per measure, 10 deep method reads, plus a gap-critic round that added 4 fields (medical case difficulty, human factors/HRA, learning theory/machine teaching, comparative cognition). ~120 subagent runs across ~20 sharded workflows (including two API-capacity reruns). All raw structured outputs preserved under notes/raw/; empirical checks in analysis/.
