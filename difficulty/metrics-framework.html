The underlying picture, in words: for each model, plot its per-task failure rates against task difficulty. Every candidate metric is one number read off that cloud. Capability, reliability, and the third axis are three different reads of the same cloud.

The three-axis geometry (the project maintainers' framing, adopted)

From the literal: "capability and reliability are not opposite ends... capability is kind of like an average, mean, median... statistic. Reliability is on one hand. We could create a name for the third thing... the other extreme end."

  • Reliability, the lower extreme: the difficulty up to which the model essentially does not fail (anchor τ = 1%).
  • Capability, the central statistic: where the model's failure transitions through even odds (anchor τ = 50%; the project maintainers' adopted choice, made by looking at the failure-trend plots: "I just chose that and I think it was kind of a good choice").
  • The third axis (name to be earned; "brilliance is not a good name"), the upper extreme: the difficulty beyond which the model essentially never succeeds; the project maintainers' anti-fluke instinct: "twice-solved tasks, at least twice."

All three are difficulty-valued level statistics of one curve. The gaps between them are then themselves meaningful (transition width below and above the center: a model that "quickly transitions" vs one "struggling persistently", the project maintainers' words; note the two gaps need not be equal, and their asymmetry is a candidate diagnostic, not assumed away).

The axioms: each with its literal source and its operational test

A1: Equation first, counting preferred. Source: "there should be some very simple math equation here: what are we calculating?" / "the former has this simple kind of counting way". Statement: a candidate IS its defining equation over (f_mt, n, d_t, w_t); one display equation, notation from the table, no free prose terms. A preference ladder applies when candidates tie on other axioms: plain sum/count > order statistic > fitted-curve read-off. Test: the equation card exists and a reader can recompute the value from the CSV with no further choices.

A2: Composition stability (task addition moves precision, not the estimand). Source: "should be stable as you add more tasks... Adding more tests only changes how well we discriminate." Statement: under enlargement of the task set by tasks drawn from the same difficulty distribution, the candidate's estimand is unchanged; under deliberate stocking attacks it must not be purchasable. Tests (identical for all candidates): (i) task bootstrap: resample tasks with replacement, report the estimand's spread; (ii) the duplication attack: duplicate the easiest quartile, report median shift across 46 configs; (iii) source-half swap: each half of wave 1 alone vs pooled, report rank agreement of the 46 configs.

A3: Difficulty-space residence. Source: "it should live in the difficulty space somehow... whatever score it is, it should still be difficulty-based", or else "some natural meaning". Statement: the value is a difficulty (d-units, board failure on the difficulty scale) or carries a defended natural unit with a stated conversion story. Counts conditioned on pool size are out (the project maintainers' verdict on the mastered count: "so conditioned on the number of tasks, and it's not in difficulty"), unless re-expressed in difficulty or axis units.

A4: No arbitrary constants. Source: "why 44? It seems quite arbitrary." / "that 5 is trash". Statement: the only free anchors are the adopted thresholds (1%, 50%) and declared protocol facts (n = 128, the verifier). Any other constant must be derived, or shown by sweep to not drive conclusions, or it kills the candidate.

A5: New-coordinate requirement. Source: the project maintainers' repeated objection that candidates "look quite linear in the mean pass rate... almost like a linear transformation of that." Statement: a candidate must carry information beyond the incumbent mean pass rate and beyond the already-accepted pair (50%-crossing, reliable range). Test: Spearman rank correlation with mean pass rate across the 46 configs, and residual spread after conditioning on the accepted pair; report both. Near-monotone (|ρ| ≳ 0.98) fails unless the candidate's case is explicitly units/interpretation rather than new ordering, and then it must say so on its card (the project maintainers' praise-with-suspicion of the surprisal metric is the template for honest labeling).

A6: Graceful saturation (the catastrophic case). Source: "The catastrophic failure is if it's way too easy and the model doesn't fail, almost never." Statement: for a model that never fails on the task set (and one that never succeeds), the candidate returns a defined, bounded, one-sidedly honest value ("≥ hardest measured difficulty", "no range"), never an infinity, an undefined expression, or a silently extrapolated number. Test: evaluate on synthetic all-pass and all-fail models plus the board's near-saturated configs; inspect values and intervals.

A7: Protocol robustness (n-dependence and flukes). Source: the project maintainers' twice-solved instinct; the candidates page's measured 0.27-probability mastery miscount at n=128. Statement: the candidate states how it changes when n changes; estimand drift under attempt-subsampling (n = 32) must be small or explicitly n-indexed; single-success flukes must not move upper-extreme candidates (anti-fluke condition stated on the card: ≥2 successes or a posterior form). Test: attempt-subsample at n = 32, report estimand shift per config.

A8: Generalization promise. Source: "somehow promising to generalize"; the project maintainers' worry: "whether any scaling trends could be picked up from it... could end up doing exactly the scaling thing." Statement (soft axiom, reported not pass/fail): the candidate should show (i) cross-benchmark consistency (its two source-half readings of the same model agree up to stated uncertainty); (ii) sane scaling-ladder behavior; (iii) no self-fulfillment: the capability-vs-reliability gradient across models must not be an artifact of task mix (test: recompute the gradient under reweighted pools; the project maintainers' stated comfort condition is a gradient near 1 that survives reweighting).

Estimator honesty (constraints inherited from the estimator roles)

Two measured facts from the maintainers bind every candidate's uncertainty story: (1) interval calibration: fitted-crossing intervals computed under binomial noise cover the truth 17–26% of the time at a promised 95%, because within-task attempts are correlated; until then, no definitional conclusion in this program may rest on raw band widths; comparisons use task-level bootstrap spreads. (2) The fitted/counting division of labor: fitted trends pool evidence (precise at the center, defined at levels counting cannot see) but extrapolate shape below the pooled evidence floor (~0.01% failure) and carry measured end-window artifacts; counting constructions are granular but honest at deep levels (the pooled 1% boundary genuinely resolves where the fitted crossing partly extrapolates). Consequence for candidates: central-statistic candidates may use fitted reads; extreme-statistic candidates (both tails) should be counting-based or state their extrapolation dependence on the card. Two further inherited rules: fit counts by likelihood, never fitted rates by least squares; and at this board's effective information (~1.7 independent attempts per task after correlation) no estimator can certify structure a straight line does not already carry; a candidate whose case requires richer structure must say it needs a bigger board.

Measured update: at the 1% level, per-model fitted crossings carry ~3× the estimation noise of a pooled-shape implied crossing (mean |LOO error| 0.512 vs 0.167 on the difficulty scale; 5/46 censored vs 0/46): most per-model scatter off the capability line at deep levels is estimator noise, not model idiosyncrasy. Replicated (preliminary) on both independent benchmarks: 5× on GSM8K-Platinum, 3.8× on CRUXEval-O, zero censoring for the pooled coordinate: a three-board constraint. Consequence: deep-level coordinates should be counting-based or pooled-shape-based, not per-model-fitted. Standing caution carried literal: predictivity rewards smooth-in-location coordinates, so a pooled coordinate HIDES real per-model idiosyncrasy by construction if it exists; that question stays open (their per-model scale detector is designed, unbuilt) and candidate cards must state both readings.

Direct evidence for the axis geometry (reliability-why's matched-pairs measurement, cited not restated): at matched mean pass rate (within 1 point), all-32-attempts-succeed probability differs by up to ~19 points across models; capability explains R²≈0.9 of it with residual spread ≈0.09: reliability carries real information beyond capability, which is exactly the A5 bar the third axis must also clear against BOTH.

Amendment 1: ownership and tail-weighting (added after the Phase-2

generators launched, before any test-suite run; recorded for pre-registration

integrity)

the maintainers's decomposition adds three binding facts. (1) A metric is owned by someone: at matched capability, the reliability residual on the 1%-crossing is only ~34% a stable model property (CI 0–69%); 44–51% is a reproducible model×benchmark interaction and 13–19% task lottery. A tail-anchored third axis defined as a pure model coordinate therefore has a measured ceiling. Test-suite addition: every candidate gets an ownership test: its cross-benchmark trait share (the two source-half readings of the same model) is measured and printed on the card; candidates must declare whether they claim to be a model coordinate or a (model, pool) coordinate. (2) Tail-weighting must be declared: the trait-vs-pair split of a summary moves with how deeply it weights the tail (tail-weighted → pair-dominated; pool-averaged → trait-dominated), so each card states its tail weighting explicitly. (3) One measured candidate enters the task set from their lane: the excess count of failed easy tasks beyond what capability predicts, near-constant across benchmarks (0.89–0.91) and predictive out-of-pool, noting it points BELOW capability ("leakiness"), a different direction than the hard-end reach the project maintainers sketched; whether the third axis should be reach (the project maintainers' instinct) or leakiness (their portable quantity), or whether reach survives the ownership test at all, is now an explicit Phase-3/4 question, not an assumption either way. adopted out as axis material by their leave-one-out work: family lineage, failure-fingerprint entropy alone, per-task failure depth, thinking flags, size; and (added with their clash-content result) failure-category profiles at model level: a model's mix of failure kinds bears zero relation to its reliability residual (corr² = 0.00), even though individual clash cells lean interpretation-flavored (their /h/clash-content.html): flavor lives in cells, not in models.

Amendment 2: twelve loopholes closed (from the adversarial probe, with

measured demonstrations; added before any test-suite run)

The cross-cutting generator's adversarial arm attacked the axioms as written and produced twelve exploits, several demonstrated numerically on the board. Each is closed as follows, and the demonstrations join the regression tests:

  1. A5 near-determinism kill: a candidate that is a near-deterministic function of already-accepted coordinates FAILS regardless of its ρ vs mean pass (demo: 2·c50 − range has ρ 0.478 yet residual sd 0.000 on the pair). Kill bar: R² > 0.99 against the accepted pair.
  2. A5 replication requirement: residual "new information" must replicate across TASK split-halves (the generalization unit is tasks, not attempts; demo: a singleton order statistic showed residual sd 0.70 with task-split replication r = 0.158: noise posing as signal). Report r; below 0.5 the novelty claim fails.
  3. A3 laundering door closed: monotone re-expression into difficulty units does not lift a ban: a difficulty-valued candidate that is rank-identical to a banned quantity inherits the ban (demo: d-of-M at Spearman 1.00 with the banned mastered count). Regression test: rank correlation against the banned bank (pool counts, mastered counts).
  4. A4 necessity test: "derived from protocol facts" is not enough: the constant bank is dense (⌈τT⌉=5, ⌈√T⌉=22, T/10=44 are all "derivable" and materially different metrics). A derived constant must be FORCED by the construction (unique, or invariant under the sweep); otherwise it is a knob and kills.
  5. A2 reporting upgraded: median shift alone masks directional purchase (demo: an ε-floored vote reads median 0.000 while 80% of configs move deeper, p90 +0.13). The test suite reports signed fraction moved, p75/p90/max, and adds NON-PUBLIC attacks chosen at test suite time (mid-pool stocking; targeted stocking just above a rival's boundary) plus the second composition axis: leave-one-family-out on the MODEL roster (metrics may lean on the population beyond d_t).
  6. A6 censoring propagation: typed censored values never silently enter downstream statistics; every A5/A8 number prints its defined-subset (this pool upper-censors 12/46 configs for width/gap candidates, a pool-ceiling fact that must stay visible).
  7. A7 tightened: "n-indexed" is admissible only with a stated n→∞ limit object or a posterior companion; the anti-fluke clause binds ANY candidate that moves when singleton successes are zeroed (the δ discount-twin audit runs on every card, not just self-declared upper-extreme ones).
  8. A1 made literal: every card ships a ~10-line reference implementation; an independent agent reimplements from the equation alone and must match to stated tolerance; the preference-ladder class is assigned by the reimplementer, not the proposer.
  9. A8 forking-paths guard: cards declare their FULL variant set (gates, τ pairs, weight forms) up front; the test suite runs all declared variants; presenting a post-hoc selected variant kills the card.
  10. Jeffreys-laundering ban: the ½ pseudocount is protocol, not a scale-setting device (demo: mean smoothed failure on the difficulty scale = the mastered count laundered, r −0.97 with zero-failure count via the −5.55 pseudocount floor). Regression test added.
  11. Witness independence: anti-fluke conditions must be vote-mass based, not task-count based: near-duplicate tasks echo one fluke into "≥2 tasks" conditions (demo: dup-hard moves an anti-fluked upper boundary up to 1.58 stepsog-odds). The near-duplicate echo attack joins the attack set.
  12. Attack-set secrecy: the published attacks are the floor, not the ceiling; the test suite includes attacks not named in this document, authored fresh by an adversary agent against the specific finalists.

Amendment 3: shared-infrastructure rules (added AFTER the test suite and

the 14-attack adversarial round, from measured results; the first

amendment informed by outcomes rather than anticipation, and marked so)

The adversarial round's structural finding: the exploitable surface is the shared difficulty-axis machinery, not candidate logic. Binding rules:

  1. Smeared-form requirement. Every difficulty-space metric ships in definition-grade form: smeared votes / position draws over the axis's own label-noise posterior (per-task se: median 0.026 d, max 0.156). Point-vote plug-ins are display/exploration only. Measured basis: the clean-twin sandwich moves the plug-in debt-free boundary median +1.39 d / max 4.1; the smeared form under the identical attack moves median 0.022 d (~60× reduction) because ε-precise position is unobservable at the measured label noise.
  2. Typed censoring extends to level-set measures. A gate still ON at a pool edge makes the value a lower bound and must be flagged (measured: 13/46 configs' Z was silently truncated; the flag carried ~a third of its headline residual).
  3. LOO-axis audit column. d_t is computed from the scored models, so every metric partially grades itself. The leave-one-model-out axis delta prints on every card (measured p90 range across adopted candidates: 0.08–0.60 d on the 47-board, 2026-08-27; a 1/roster effect that shrinks as the project grows).
  4. Pool-edge policy. Frontier values are typed pool-capped; truncation-debt diagnostics (closed form) accompany integral reads; the strip between the task set's max and the Jeffreys ceiling (logit((128+½)/129) = +5.55) is simultaneously the ceiling-attack surface and the hard-task acquisition target.
  5. EB-profile gating ban. Per-model empirical-Bayes profiles may smooth estimates, never set guarantee thresholds: profile-coupled bars invert dominance (measured: a strictly better model certified NOTHING while its dominated twin certified a range).
  6. Promoted public attacks (now regression tests): split-task threshold tipping; clean-twin sandwich; ceiling outpost/lever; LOO-axis self-reference; single-label relabel within noise; vote-siphon; endogenous flaky-frontier stocking.

The pre-registered test suite (identical for every candidate)

  1. Equation card (A1 format), then prose, then pros/cons, in that order.
  2. Computed values for all 46 configs on the canonical 442-task matrix.
  3. A2 tests: bootstrap spread; duplication attack; mix swap.
  4. A5 tests: ρ vs mean pass; residual vs (crossing-50, reliable range).
  5. A6 saturation probes; A7 n=32 subsample.
  6. Budget-transfer witness where applicable (mean failure of the last-10 tasks inside any cumulative construction's range vs its τ; inherited protocol; known result: severe at loose τ, mild at 1%).
  7. One model-point plot on the difficulty axis (project rules; the blessed plot family).
  8. Verdict against A1–A8 + the preference ladder; kill or keep.

Convergence target: at most two surviving candidates per axis, then the curated trio with the open choices (threshold anchors, third-axis name and final form) stated as decisions for the project maintainers, not made silently.

Failure modes already adopted (regression tests, not to be re-proposed)

From the project maintainers' review of the five-candidate round: arbitrary constants (44 hardest, 1280-attempt budget, 5 candidates of anything); metrics discussed before defined; counts conditioned on pool size; near-monotone-in-mean-pass presented as new coordinates; and from the corpus: equal-vote composition dependence (the task set's stocking buys credit) and budget transfer at loose thresholds (measured: a 20%-budget cumulative range admits 80% failure on its last tasks: the loose-τ cumulative family is structurally suspect for CAPABILITY even though the 1% cumulative form is sound for RELIABILITY). Killed candidates from the prior round's adversarial process (majority count, hardest-of-four, attempts-to-first-solve, own-order range, Guttman rank, reach-area, self-normalized median depth) stay killed unless a candidate materially differs and says how.

What is already accepted (the fixed points candidates build against)

  • Board difficulty d_t: population-based, accepted as reasonable ("I think that's a reasonable assumption").
  • The reliable range (cumulative ≤1%, smeared axis votes): "a reasonable way to measure reliability": reasonable-not-adopted; it anchors the lower extreme and its machinery (votes, posteriors, boundary walls) is inherited.
  • The 50%-crossing as capability: the project maintainers' adopted anchor and current suspicion ("maybe the current definition of difficulty at a 50% failure rate IS a robust definition of capabilities"): the null hypothesis capability candidates must beat or formalize, not a straw man.
  • The averages mechanism (Jeffreys-flavored smoothing, models that transition quickly showing reliability near capability): liked; keep visible.
